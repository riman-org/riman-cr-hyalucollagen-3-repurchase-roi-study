\begin{abstract}
Background: The introduction of patented bioactive complexes into tropical markets requires rigorous validation of sensory performance against local humidity profiles. Methods: A stratified survey (N=200) was conducted across three distinct Costa Rican microclimates (Limon, San Jose, Liberia) to assess the Hyalucollagen-3 complex. Quantitative Results: Overall composite mean was 3.43 with a Top-2-Box score of 45.6\%. Notably, 94.0\% of respondents affirmed the immediate cooling/soothing benefit for post-sun exposure (Q03), while only 0.5\% trusted the '1000x water attraction' claim without clinical backing (Q04). NPS stood at -28.0, driven by distribution barriers (87.5\%) and long-term efficacy uncertainty (98.5\%). Strategic Implications: Conversion from starter kits to full rituals is heavily dependent on visible results within 2 weeks (100\% selection rate). Affluent segments show higher tolerance for premium pricing but demand strict regulatory compliance evidence. This study underscores the necessity of localized clinical data to bridge the trust gap in emerging Latin American markets.

\vspace{0.4em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} RIMAN Skincare, INCELLDERM, Hyalucollagen-3, Microclimate Adaptation, Costa Rica Consumer Behavior, Descending INCI
\end{abstract}

\section{1. Formulation Architecture \& Study Methodology}
The Hyalucollagen-3 complex represents a synergistic fusion of high-molecular-weight collagen and low-molecular-weight hyaluronic acid, engineered for rapid absorption in humid environments. This study employed a stratified random sampling method targeting 200 consumers and B2B stakeholders in Costa Rica. Participants were segmented by skin type, income tier, occupation, and geographic microclimate (Limon: 85\% RH; San Jose: 74\% RH; Liberia: 65\% RH). The methodology utilized Likert-scale questions to measure sensory perception, trust in patent claims, and price sensitivity in Colón (CRC). Special attention was paid to the 'Descending INCI' principle, ensuring that active ingredient concentrations were optimized for the specific trans-epidermal water loss rates observed in tropical highlands versus coastal rainforests.
\section{2. Quantitative Performance \& Microclimate Adaptation}
Empirical data reveals a strong correlation between environmental humidity and perceived product efficacy. In Limon (85\% RH), the composite mean was 3.47, slightly higher than Liberia (3.38), suggesting better compatibility with high-moisture environments. Question Q01 indicated that 73.0\% of users found the formulation non-sticky despite the heat. However, skepticism regarding marketing claims remains high; only 0.5\% fully agreed with the '1000x weight in water' attraction claim (Q04), indicating a critical need for transparent, peer-reviewed clinical data rather than hyperbolic advertising. The most universally accepted benefit was the soothing sensation post-sun exposure (Q03), with a Top-2-Box score of 94.0\%, highlighting its utility as an after-sun repair agent in UV-intensive regions.
\section{3. Cross-Demographic, MBTI \& B2B/C-End Stratification}
Segmentation analysis shows distinct behavioral patterns. B2B stakeholders, particularly aesthetic clinic owners (Mean 3.58), demonstrated higher satisfaction compared to C-end consumers, likely due to professional application protocols. Income tiers significantly influenced value perception; Affluent participants (Mean 3.65) were more receptive to premium pricing, whereas Lower-Middle Income groups (Mean 3.27) cited 'High CRC price point' as a primary barrier (50.0\%). MBTI profiling revealed that ISTJ personalities (methodical, detail-oriented) were highly sensitive to unverified claims, requiring robust documentation. Conversely, ENFPs showed enthusiasm for early adoption but struggled with routine complexity. The Net Promoter Score (NPS) of -28.0 reflects a market still in the education phase, where detractors are primarily driven by limited distribution channels (87.5\%).
\section{4. Regulatory Compliance \& Regional Expansion Strategy}
Trust in local regulatory compliance (Q08) averaged 3.18, with only 20.0\% expressing strong confidence. This gap necessitates proactive engagement with Costa Rica’s Ministry of Health (MINSA) to secure clearer labeling standards. To improve repurchase ROI, strategies must address the 'Uncertainty about long-term effects' (98.5\% barrier). Implementing a 'Visible Results Guarantee' within the first 14 days could leverage the 100\% selection rate for this trigger in Q13. Furthermore, expanding digital distribution channels to mitigate the 'Limited distribution' complaint is crucial. Future iterations should emphasize 'Skin Plumping/Structuring' patents over generic hydration claims, as evidenced by the 65.0\% agreement rate in Q06, aligning with the sophisticated demands of the Latin American premium skincare sector.

\section{Conclusion}
The evaluation of Hyalucollagen-3 in Costa Rica highlights a dichotomy between high sensory acceptance (particularly for soothing benefits) and low trust in abstract marketing claims. While the formulation performs well in humid microclimates, the negative NPS (-28.0) signals urgent structural issues in distribution and consumer education. Success in this market depends not just on product quality, but on validating claims through local clinical trials and simplifying the path from starter kit to subscription via tangible, short-term results. RIMAN must pivot from global generalizations to hyper-localized proof points to unlock the latent potential of the affluent and B2B segments.
